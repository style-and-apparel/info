The Role of Suspenders and Braces in Fashion


Suspenders and braces, often used interchangeably, hold an intriguing place in the world of fashion. These accessories, which serve the practical purpose of holding up trousers, have evolved significantly over the years and have carved out a niche that combines utility with style. Understanding the history and evolution of suspenders and braces provides insight into their role in fashion today.


The origin of suspenders dates back to the 18th century, when they were first used primarily as undergarments. They were not intended to be seen and were often simple straps attached to trousers with buttonholes. The early 19th century saw the popularization of suspenders in Britain, largely due to the influence of Albert Thurston, who began manufacturing them in 1820. These early suspenders were designed to be worn over the shoulders, crossing at the back, and were typically made of ribbon or woven fabric. As trousers during this period were high-waisted and lacked belt loops, suspenders became an essential part of a gentlemans wardrobe.


In the United States, suspenders gained popularity in the mid-19th century. They were often seen as a symbol of practicality and were adopted by working-class men for their functionality. However, as the century progressed, suspenders began to be associated with a more refined aesthetic, becoming a staple in formal attire. This transition marked the beginning of suspenders as a fashion statement, not just a practical accessory.


The term braces is more commonly used in the UK, and while it refers to the same accessory, there are some nuanced differences. Braces are typically seen as more formal and are often associated with traditional British tailoring. They are usually made from high-quality materials like silk or jacquard, and are considered a hallmark of classic British style. In contrast, suspenders in the US have often been more casual, made from elastic or other more utilitarian materials, though they too have their place in formalwear.


The evolution of these accessories has seen them transition from purely functional items to fashion statements. The 1920s and 1930s saw a decline in their popularity as belts became more common, largely because trousers waistlines began to drop. Nevertheless, suspenders and braces never fully vanished from the fashion scene. In the latter half of the 20th century, they experienced a resurgence, particularly in the 1960s and 1980s, when subcultures such as skinheads and punks adopted them as part of their aesthetic. This period saw suspenders being worn in more unconventional ways, often with a nod to rebellion and nonconformity.


Today, suspenders and braces have found a balance between their historical roots and modern fashion. They are embraced by both high fashion designers and everyday fashion enthusiasts. Their versatility allows them to be paired with everything from three-piece suits to casual jeans and a shirt. In many ways, they have become a symbol of timeless style, appreciated for both their functional and aesthetic contributions to fashion.


In conclusion, suspenders and braces have a rich history that reflects broader changes in fashion and society. From their origins as hidden undergarments to their status as iconic fashion accessories, they have evolved while maintaining their fundamental purpose. Whether referred to as suspenders or braces, these accessories continue to play a significant role in fashion, bridging the gap between practical utility and sartorial elegance.
